Data

This run used a synthetic arterial-pressure cohort generated by src/synth.py. It is not patient data. Its value is that every artifact is injected at a known location, which is the only way to measure whether artifact rejection works instead of asserting that it does. MIMIC-IV Waveform and VitalDB are credentialed and are not bundled.

What the pipeline decides

Per ten-second window: is this waveform trustworthy enough to derive features from? Both errors are expensive. Admitting artifact produces confident garbage in whatever model consumes it; rejecting good signal throws away the data an early-warning model needs.

Every rule is a named check with a stated threshold rather than a learned score. A clinician asking why a window was dropped deserves a better answer than that the model said so.

The five rules

The spike rule took three attempts, and the failures are instructive

Scoring the first difference against its own median flags every systolic upstroke, because an arterial upstroke genuinely is the largest sample-to-sample change in a clean window. That version rejected 99% of good data.

Subtracting a five-sample running median does not fix it: the median lags a fast upstroke, so each beat leaves ten samples of large residual and a clean ten-second window scores about two hundred "spikes".

What separates the two is direction, not magnitude. A pressure upstroke is monotone — consecutive differences share a sign for the whole rise. A spike goes up and comes straight back down. Requiring a sign reversal and a magnitude above the window's robust scale isolates transients without touching the pulse, which is what moved rejection F1 from 0.045 to 0.904.

Threshold sensitivity

Which rule is worth sweeping

This sweep moved twice while the pipeline was built. Sweeping the SQI threshold gave a flat line, because SQI fires on 16 of 1440 windows here. So did sweeping the spike threshold, once the spike detector worked — a detector that separates the classes cleanly makes its own threshold stop mattering.

Step-change is what binds on this cohort, so that is what is swept. Which rule binds is a property of the data, not a constant, and on real records it will move again. A sweep over an inactive rule looks like a sensitivity analysis and tells you nothing.

cd physiological-waveform-pipeline

What this does not establish

  • 4.0 synthetic waveform-hours. Real ICU records contain failure modes this generator does not model, and the rejection scores above will not survive contact with them unchanged.
  • The generator and the detector were written by the same person. Scoring a detector on artifacts you chose to inject is a necessary first step and not an external validation.
  • ABP only. No ECG, PPG, respiratory or capnography channels.
  • No clinical outcome is attached to any window here, so nothing in this project speaks to patient benefit.
