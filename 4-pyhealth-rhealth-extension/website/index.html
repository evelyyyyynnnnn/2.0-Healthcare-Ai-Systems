Data

Run on the PhysioNet MIMIC-IV clinical database demo, retrieved with per-file hashes recorded in data/MANIFEST.json. MIMIC-IV records one person across several ICU stays, which is what makes a row-level or stay-level split leak: the same patient's physiology ends up on both sides of it.

The subject-split score sits at chance. On a cohort this small the model has learned nothing that generalises to a new person, and the row-split figure was entirely leakage. Both halves are reported together: the direction is a real methodological finding, the magnitude depends on cohort size and readmission structure and must not be quoted as a general rate.

37 readmissions across 97 people is what makes a stay-level split almost as leaky as a row-level one. Grouping by stay feels careful and is not enough; only grouping by the person is.

The guards that ship with it

GuardFired correctlyWhat it refuses
Subject appearing in both splitsyesa split whose train and test sides share a subject_id
Row with an event already activeyespredicting an event that is already under way
Row without full follow-upyesa label that could not have been observed

These are assertions a pipeline can call, not conventions someone has to remember. icuflow check-split exits non-zero rather than scoring a leaky split. The package is installable from this repository and is not published, so it has no downloads and no users.
